# Claude leg 2: the 15 disputed points

Written 25/11/2026. Everything below was read or run on 25/11/2026 between 16:09 and 16:25 CET, on this Mac (macOS 26.6.2, build 25G83, `sw_vers`).

## New measurement: system WebKit, windowless

Probe: a 100-line Swift AppKit binary (`/tmp/claude2-probe.lv4F/probe.swift`, runner `run.py`), activation policy `.prohibited`, every WKWebView 900x1000 and never placed in any window, pages served by a `vcx://` WKURLSchemeHandler, the same synthetic pages as round 1 (5,000 rows, 72,006 elements as a full DOM, 221 virtualized). Memory is `footprint -p` of the probe and of every `com.apple.WebKit.*` process whose responsible PID is the probe, read 4 s after every view finished loading. Engine: the system WebKit.framework, CFBundleVersion 21624.5.1.11.3.

- 1 blank view: app 17 MB, WebContent 9, GPU 6, Networking 6; total 38 MB.
- 1 virtualized transcript: WebContent 32 MB (Playwright WebKit in round 1: 49). 1 full DOM: WebContent 223 MB (Playwright: 316).
- 8 views, a new configuration each, default store, virtualized: 8 WebContent processes of 32 to 33 MB; total with app 23, GPU 14, Networking 6: 300 MB (two runs, 300 and 300).
- 8 views sharing one WKWebViewConfiguration object and one `WKWebsiteDataStore.nonPersistent()`: 8 WebContent processes, total 299 MB. Blank pages: 8 processes of about 10 MB, total 109 MB.
- 1 opener + 7 views opened by `window.open`, answered in `createWebViewWith` with the configuration WebKit supplies: 1 WebContent process of 133 MB, total 175 and 176 MB (two runs). Blank pages: 1 process of 13 MB, total 43 MB. After the app called `reload()` or `load(url)` on all 8: still 1 process, 144 to 145 MB.
- 8 related views with the full DOM: 1 WebContent process of 1,595 MB, total 1,634 MB.
- Hang test: a sandboxed `srcdoc` iframe (`sandbox="allow-scripts"`) running `for(;;){}` injected into view 1, then `evaluateJavaScript("2")` on view 8. Related layout: no answer after 5,000 ms. Separate layout: answered in 0 ms.

## The 15 points

1. Process sharing. A and B hold; C and D are refuted by measurement, and C and D have both yielded. Eight views sharing one data store and even one configuration object got eight WebContent processes; only the related views got one. The tag codex-2 cites is the right one: `gh api repos/WebKit/WebKit/git/ref/tags/WebKit-7624.5.1.11.3` returns the tag, and this Mac's WebKit.framework and Safari 26.6.2 both report 21624.5.1.11.3, the same version with Apple's OS-family prefix "21". My own reading of that tag: `WebProcessPool::createWebPage` (WebProcessPool.cpp:1340 to 1369) shares a process only through `relatedPage->ensureRunningProcess()` (or the opener's process under site isolation); otherwise the launch is delayed and `processForSite(..., IsSharedProcess::No, ...)` (lines 1270 to 1323) reuses only an idle cached process, a suspended page's process, a prewarmed one, or, in `usesSingleWebProcess` mode (private SPI, `_WKProcessPoolConfiguration.h:38`), any process; else it creates a new one. Apple's "implementation-defined process limit" is `s_maxProcessCount { 400 }` (WebProcessProxy.cpp:170), and at the limit WebKit terminates the least recently used process instead of sharing (lines 294 to 298). Agreed with codex-2 on dropping "only": the private single-process mode also shares. Against my round 1: the related saving is about 125 MB for 8 windows on system WebKit, not the 200 MB my Playwright figures suggested.

2. Default process layout. Unchanged, now measured: one process per window by default. The fixed cost of a WebContent process is about 10 MB (8 blank separate views at about 10 MB each against 13 MB for 8 related), and on the synthetic transcript sharing saves 124 MB of WebContent for 8 windows. The price is also measured: a looping chart iframe in one related window froze the other seven, while separate windows kept answering. Recovery from a hung shared process means tearing down every window on it (not tested). wry adds one more cost to the related layout: a view built on its opener's configuration shares the opener's `WKUserContentController` and skips its own `ipc` handler (wry 0.58.1, src/wkwebview/mod.rs:113 to 121), so every related window's messages reach one handler and must carry a window id. Related views stay an option for when the RAM gate fails by less than their saving and a watchdog exists.

3. RAM for 8 windows. Revised down. My round-1 ranges (490 to 940 MB separate, 300 to 400 related) used Playwright's 50 MB per process; the system engine gives 32. Measured windowless and synthetic: 300 MB separate, 175 MB related, both including a bare 23 MB host. Estimate on real threads, on screen: separate 350 to 650 MB, related 230 to 450 MB, for a lean host (on-screen backing stores, images, highlighted code and charts are not in the probe). In the Swift-host steps, today's frontend (253 MB for 7 windows) comes on top until its SwiftUI transcript code leaves, so step 1 may read higher than the final layout (estimate). B's 460 to 1,080 MB is too high at the top, C's 400 to 550 fits the related case, D's 350 to 800 is plausible, E's "1.8 GB SwiftUI today" stays refuted by the brief's 253 MB.

4. Stop threshold. Unchanged at 600 MB total for 8 windows, on real threads, counting the frontend and every WebKit helper attributed to it, now with a measured basis: windowless synthetic separate views already cost 300 MB, so 600 leaves room for real content and no more. Yielded to B on energy: stop on a more than 10 % energy regression. On RAM, B's 10 % rule (about 278 MB against 253) is below the 277 MB the WebKit side alone used for 8 separate synthetic windows here, so it ends the project at step 0 by construction with the separate layout and leaves about 100 MB for host and real content with the related one. It reinstates the 19/11 council's "prove a RAM saving first" rule that the owner set aside; keep it as a target, not a stop. The baseline must be matched: today's VCX on the same 8 threads, including the one WebContent process each chart block spawns (Chart.swift:403, round 1). D's 1.2 to 1.5 GB is withdrawn by D.

5. Final shell. Unchanged on wry + tao when the shell moves; yielded to B that it is optional and last. The bare AppKit + WebKit host measured 17 to 23 MB, so a Rust shell buys no RAM; it buys only one language. wry 0.58.1 (crates.io, updated 25/11/2026) supports related views on macOS: `NewWindowResponse::Create` takes a WKWebView that "must use the same configuration as the caller webview" (src/lib.rs:461 to 470), wired in `webView:createWebViewWithConfiguration:...` (wry_web_view_ui_delegate.rs:180, 295). Tauri's stable line is 2.12.7 and a 3.0.0-alpha.6 is published (crates.io, read 25/11/2026), one more reason not to put a framework layer between VCX and WKWebView now. E's `headless-ui-test` does not exist (crates.io API: "crate `headless-ui-test` does not exist").

6. Tauri headless testing on macOS. Unchanged: A and B hold. The Tauri page (https://v2.tauri.app/develop/tests/webdriver/, "Last updated: Jun 29, 2026") says the WebdriverIO service "runs an embedded WebDriver server inside your app ... and this is how macOS is supported", while `tauri-driver` driven directly covers "only Windows and Linux ... as macOS has no WKWebView driver tool available". Windowless use stays unproven. The probe shows VCX does not need WebDriver for its checks: WKWebViews that are never in a window load, lay out and answer `evaluateJavaScript`, so the host can drive the page directly from Swift or Rust (objc2). Not tested today: `takeSnapshot` on a view with no window (VCX already snapshots views in unshown windows).

7. Composer. Unchanged on direction, yielded on the gate. My round 1 already kept the native composer through step 1 and moved it in step 2; B's condition is right and I adopt it: the composer moves into the page only after the web one passes IME with marked text (`isComposing`), image paste, attachment chips, shortcuts and VoiceOver checks. The web composer stays the goal, because a native composer beside a web transcript keeps the native and web focus boundary where today's focus theft lives (brief). No new measurement.

8. Markdown. Unchanged: parse and sanitize in the core. B's precision is right and binds my design: pulldown-cmark emits `Event::Html` and `Event::InlineHtml` (https://docs.rs/pulldown-cmark/latest/pulldown_cmark/enum.Event.html, cited by B), and those must be escaped as text, never passed through; link targets are validated in the core.

9. Order of migration. Unchanged; Muse's spike-first is refuted. Her spike exists to learn whether and how windows share a process before building on a "wrong process assumption". That question took one 100-line Swift probe and under an hour today, windowless, on the system WebKit, and the answer does not depend on the shell: the related layout is a `createWebViewWith` answer, available in Swift and in wry alike. Process layout is a host decision the template and transcript never see, so a Swift web transcript cannot be built on the wrong one. What stays unknown (footprint on screen with real threads, frame pacing while streaming, IME) is what my step 0 inside the Swift app measures, in the same 3 to 5 days, and it leaves a shippable artifact. A wry spike would answer shell questions (menus, focus, hotkeys, the shared `ipc` handler of point 2) that matter only at the last step.

10. Who applies a template edit. Unchanged: automatic after a staged offscreen test render, with last-good and built-in fallbacks; C has yielded. New from the hang test: the test render must run in a separate, unrelated web view with a hang timeout. A looping candidate then freezes only the test view; applied live to related windows, it would freeze all of them at once.

11. `vcx.live` and chart frames. Unchanged: brokered by the core with an allowlist. I did not measure it; the HTML sandbox rule (no `allow-same-origin` gives an opaque origin) and the Fetch CORS rules cited by B and C hold, so E's plain `fetch` works only against servers that answer `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *` or `null`. The hang test adds one fact: sandboxed `srcdoc` iframes run inside their page's WebContent process on this build (one process for 8 related views with a looping iframe), so chart code shares the window's failure domain.

12. Chart library. Unchanged: vendor a pinned lightweight-charts build with its attribution. I did not re-read TradingView's pages; B's and C's reads of the product comparison stand, and nothing contradicts them.

13. GPUI. Unchanged. crates.io read today: `gpui` max and stable version 0.2.2. All legs agree it cannot show agent HTML charts or a hot-reloaded HTML template, which settles it for this display whatever its maturity.

14. Effort. Unchanged: 11 to 16 developer-weeks with agents, estimate. Today's findings move parts both ways: no separate spike is needed, and a separate-process default avoids a shared-process recovery path, while a per-window watchdog stays useful. The common ground is now about 10 to 16 weeks (D raised to 10 to 16, B 9 to 16 without a host); C's 14 to 20 includes a draft-approval flow and a separate hardening step that C has since dropped.

15. One shared WKWebView with tabs. Unchanged: one window per session. Muse's argument that tabs are "the only layout guaranteed to use one WebContent process" is beside the point once related views measured one process for 8 windows. A tab mode would sit near the 1-window figure (69 MB total, synthetic) but shows one session at a time and changes how he works; it stays a later idea only if every window layout fails the gate.

## Do the round-1 Playwright numbers transfer?

In ratio, yes; in absolute value, no. For the same pages, Playwright's WebKit build 2368 used about 1.5 times the system WebKit's memory: 49 against 32 MB virtualized, 316 against 223 MB full DOM, 194 against 133 MB for 8 related views, 400 against 257 MB of WebContent for 8 separate views (I did not measure the cause; Playwright ships its own WebKit build). The ratios hold in both engines: related costs about half the WebContent of separate, and a full DOM about 7 times a virtualized one. Both runs are windowless and synthetic: no on-screen backing stores, no display-link frames, no images or highlighted code. What it changes: RAM estimates drop by about a third, a separate process per window becomes affordable as the default, the related saving shrinks to about 125 MB for 8 windows, and virtualization stays mandatory (8 related full-DOM views reached 1.63 GB). The on-screen figure must still be read; the way to read it without opening a window on his desktop is B's: ship the web transcript behind a setting and read `footprint` of VCX and its attributed WebKit helpers during his normal use.

## Recommendation

1. Start now with the web transcript inside today's Swift app (step 0 on a real long thread, 3 to 5 days); no separate wry spike, since system WebKit already answered the process question (8 separate views 300 MB, 8 related 175 MB, synthetic, windowless).
2. One WKWebView per window in its own WebContent process by default; related views only if the RAM gate misses by less than their roughly 125 MB saving and a watchdog exists, because one looping chart froze all eight related windows.
3. Stop above 600 MB total for 8 windows on real threads (frontend plus every attributed WebKit helper, read during his normal use) or on a more than 10 % energy regression; B's 10 % RAM rule is a target, not a stop.
4. Keep the native composer until the web one passes IME, paste, attachment, shortcut and VoiceOver checks; template edits go live automatically after a test render in a separate view with a hang timeout, with last-good and built-in fallbacks.
5. Then Rust workers behind the socket protocol, the Rust core, and a wry + tao shell last and only if wanted (a bare host costs 17 to 23 MB, so the shell language buys no RAM); 11 to 16 developer-weeks with agents, estimate.
